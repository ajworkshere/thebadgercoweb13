Problem Areas / Search & Fit Intent

Common shoe friction points
and what they mean.

Fit Check helps you distinguish between true length issues and internal volume or shape mismatches.

Heel Mechanics

Heel Slipping & Lift

A slipping heel does not automatically mean the shoe is too long. Excess internal volume around the rearfoot, a wide heel counter, or loose midfoot lacing can cause the foot to lift with each step. Sizing down often causes forefoot pinching without fixing the heel contour.

Read the heel slipping guide →
Forefoot & Toes

Toe-Box Pressure & Tight Forefoot

Shoes can feel cramped at the toes even when the length is correct. A rapid toe-box taper, low vertical clearance (toe ceiling), or a narrow ball width will pinch metatarsals. Automatically sizing up adds unwanted length and can cause heel slippage.

Read the toe-box pressure guide →
Instep & Closure

Lace Pressure & Instep Tightness

Sharp pressure across the bridge of your foot often reflects an instep-to-vamp volume mismatch. In closed-lacing styles (such as oxford dress shoes), high arches can prevent the facing from closing properly, creating excessive downward pressure.

Explore shoe anatomy →
Friction & Materials

Shoe Rubbing & Collar Irritation

Rubbing at the ankle bone or Achilles tendon is often caused by collar height, stiff leather counters, or a break-in period that needs gradual adaptation. Fit Check helps you identify whether adjustments, padding, or rest are appropriate.

Inside Your Fit Check Result

Five clear parts.
Nuanced next steps.

Fit Check provides clear, structured advice based on the specifics of your footwear and reported symptoms.

Advice never assumes every dress shoe is narrow or that sizing up is always the cure. It separates what you can adjust from what is inherent to the shoe's construction.

01 / Observation

What we noticed

Clear reflection of your answers and how the shoe's shape, volume, or lacing relates to your reported issue.

02 / Action

Try first

The lowest-risk adjustment to test—such as alternative lacing, tongue pad placement, or sock thickness.

03 / Tools

Helpful Tools

Supportive fitting aids, non-diagnostic cushion pads, or shoe trees when relevant to the construction.

04 / Caution

What to avoid

Practices that worsen fit—such as overstretching stiff leathers or aggressive overtightening across the instep.

05 / Boundary

Safety guidance

Escalation criteria advising when to stop wearing the pair and seek evaluation from a healthcare professional.

Fit Check FAQ

Common questions.

Does Fit Check require a photo or foot scan?

No. Fit Check works entirely through structured questions regarding your footwear and your reported experience. No camera or scan is required.

What footwear styles can I evaluate?

Fit Check is optimized for dress shoes (oxfords, derbies, loafers), dress boots (chelsea, chukka), and everyday lifestyle sneakers.

What is the difference between Fit Check and Foot Check?

Fit Check is specific to one shoe pair and its construction. Foot Check evaluates broader foot discomfort, standing fatigue, and daily routine factors across all shoes.

Does Fit Check diagnose medical conditions?

No. Fit Check is an educational footwear fit tool. It offers non-diagnostic fit adjustments and safety escalation advice when symptoms warrant professional care.
